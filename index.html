    How tax is handled
    • Federal and provincial tax use 2026 brackets and credits for all 13 provinces and territories: basic personal, age, pension income and CPP/EI, plus Ontario's surtax and Health Premium, BC's tax reduction, Manitoba's basic amount phase-out, and Quebec's abatement and family-income credits. Brackets are assumed to rise with inflation.
    • Checked in October 2026: brackets and credits for every province against CRA, provincial and TaxTips figures, and combined marginal rates against a published BC tax card.
    • OAS clawback starts at $95,323 of net income. Pension splitting picks the split that saves the most each year.
    • RRSP withdrawals from 65 are treated as RRIF income, so they qualify for the pension credit and splitting.
    • Extra RRSP money not needed for spending goes into TFSA room first, then non-registered. Estate tax assumes the remaining RRIF is taxed in one final return.
    • When a spouse dies, RRSP/RRIF, TFSA and non-registered money roll to the survivor tax-free. OAS stops, pension splitting ends, and the survivor gets the CPP survivor's pension (capped with their own CPP) plus the $2,500 death benefit.
    • Taxable income events are taxed as regular income without CPP/EI. Care costs don't claim the medical expense credit yet.
    • Not yet modelled: tax on non-registered growth; low-income tax reductions outside BC (like Ontario's); Quebec's Health Services Fund contribution and QPP/QPIP payroll rates (CPP/EI rates are used); and indexation pauses in some provinces.
